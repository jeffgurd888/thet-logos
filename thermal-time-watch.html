How this clock keeps time

The dial is arc: solar longitude λ runs 0° at the March equinox to 360° around the year. Riding on the arc is the equilibrium track T_eq(λ) — the temperature the Sun’s geometry is asking of Schoolcraft, Michigan. Thermal phase Θ accumulates at a rate proportional to that temperature, dΘ/dt = T_eq / T̄, and the thirteen modules are equal steps of Θ — so winter modules stretch over more days and more arc, and summer modules compress.

The thin red hand is the thermal seconds hand. It sweeps at today’s rate r = T_eq / T̄: one thermal minute takes 60/r civil seconds. Deep January runs about 5% slow; July runs about 4.7% fast. The gold hand is the year hand — it points at today’s place on the module ring. The white hands read thermal time of day.
