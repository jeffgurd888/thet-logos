## The theorem

**The single-plaquette factor is a positive-definite class
function at general (G, ρ), for all β ≥ 0** — Route B's
foundation stone, previously a paper argument only:

- `plaquetteFactor_posDefFun` — for `0 ≤ β`,
  `PosDefFun (fun g => (Real.exp (−(β * wordDensity ρ g)) : ℂ))`,
  where `wordDensity ρ g = 1 − (tr ρ g).re / d` is the
  corpus's one-plaquette Wilson density and `PosDefFun` is
  the positive-definite-function predicate in kernel form,
  defined in this module (no owned or Mathlib predicate in
  that form existed).
- `plaquetteFactor_posDef_classFn` — the packaged statement:
  positive-definiteness AND constancy on conjugacy classes
  (`wordDensity_conj_invariant`, by trace cyclicity).

The proof route is Gram, not Peter–Weyl (the tree owns no PW
infrastructure for this): the character kernel
`g ↦ (tr ρ g).re` is positive-definite because
`tr (ρ (x⁻¹ y)) = ∑_{a,b} conj ((ρ x) a b) · (ρ y) a b`
(`trace_repMatrix_inv_mul_gram`) — a sum of squared entry
norms — and the factor is the positive power series
`e^{−β} · exp((β/d) · Re tr ρ ·)` of that kernel, closed
under products through Mathlib's Schur product theorem
(`Matrix.PosSemidef.hadamard`, as the corpus already walks
it). No `0 < d` hypothesis is needed anywhere: the exponent
identity is a field identity at every d, d = 0 included.

## A lemma-level kill, at full rank (the pack's bare product
## closure is FALSE as stated)

The brick's interface pack prescribed the bare closure
"products of arbitrary `PosDefFun` functions are PD." That
statement is **false under the landed predicate**, and the
builder killed it with a counterexample rather than forcing
it: the constant function `g ↦ Complex.I` satisfies the
real-part kernel form (its form is `I·‖∑ c‖²`, real part 0),
while its square, the constant `−1`, does not. The mismatch
is exact: `Matrix.PosSemidef` requires a Hermitian Gram
matrix; the real-part-only predicate supplies no Hermitian
clause. What landed instead is the product closure in
Hermitian-kernel form (`PosDefFun.gram_posSemidef`,
`PosDefFun.mul`, `PosDefFun.pow`, carrying the hypothesis
`f g⁻¹ = conj (f g)`), through the prescribed Hadamard step —
and the hypothesis is discharged for the character kernel
(`reTr_kernel_hermitian`). A2-1's actual theorem is
unaffected: the factor is real-valued and Hermitian, so the
memo's "positive-definite class function" holds for it in
the classical sense. The predicate's precision (real-part
form; Hermitian symmetry carried where needed) is recorded
on the module's face — future Route B bricks consume the
Hermitian-form lemmas, not the killed bare statement.
