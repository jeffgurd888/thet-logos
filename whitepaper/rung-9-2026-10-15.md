## Task 1 — Γ lift: **STANDS**

File: `lean/ThetLogos/SecondQuantGamma.lean` (992 lines, 32 theorems,
10 definitions), commit **`1bf2b26`**.

For a one-particle matrix `M` on the finite mode space (modes indexed by
`Fin d`), `Γ(M)` is constructed on the Rung-8 fermionic Fock space by the
compound-matrix (minor) formula, transported through the same
`fockEquiv`-conjugation Rung 8 used for creation/annihilation:

    (Γ(M) x) T = ∑ S, minorDet M S T * x S

Key theorems (all T1):

- `gamma_apply` — the coordinate formula above (by `rfl`).
- `gamma_one` — `Γ(1) = 1`.
- `gamma_mul` — `Γ(M * N) = Γ(M) * Γ(N)` (the homomorphism).
- `gamma_adjoint` — `⟪Γ(M) x, y⟫ = ⟪x, Γ(Mᴴ) y⟫`, i.e. `Γ(M*) = Γ(M)*`
  in the inner-product form the ladder uses for adjoints.
- `gamma_vacuum` — `Γ(M)` fixes the vacuum, for every `M` (not only
  unitaries; the vacuum is the empty-occupation vector).
- `gamma_sector` — `Γ(M)` preserves every n-particle sector, for every
  `M`. Proved via `mem_sector_iff`: sector membership is exactly
  vanishing of coordinates off the cardinality-n finsets.
- `gamma_inner` — for `Mᴴ * M = 1`, `Γ(M)` preserves the Fock inner
  product; with `gamma_comp_self` (`Γ(Mᴴ) * Γ(M) = 1`) and
  `gamma_self_comp` (`Γ(M) * Γ(Mᴴ) = 1` when `M * Mᴴ = 1`), `Γ(U)` is
  unitary for unitary `U`, as an operator identity, not only on vectors.
- `gamma_diagonal_basis` — `Γ(diag v)` acts on a basis vector by the
  product of the selected phases: `Γ(diag v) e_S = (∏ i ∈ S, v i) • e_S`.

Supporting determinant core (all T1, proved here — rectangular
Cauchy–Binet is not in Mathlib as of this toolchain):

- `minorDet` — the `(S, T)` minor of a matrix; `minorDet_apply`,
  `minorDet_of_card_ne`, `minorDet_conjTranspose`, `minorDet_one`,
  `minorDet_diagonal`.
- `det_mul_expansion` — `det (A * B) = ∑ f, (∏ i, B (f i) i) · det (A ∘ f)`
  over intermediate index functions.
- `cauchy_binet_rect` — the rectangular Cauchy–Binet formula
  `det (A * B) = ∑ R, minorCols A R * minorRows B R`, proved from the
  expansion by grouping index functions by their image and ordering
  permutation (`orderingPerm`, `orderingPerm_key`); non-injective index
  functions vanish by column duplication.
- `minorDet_mul` — `minorDet (M * N) S T = ∑ R, minorDet M R T *
  minorDet N S R`, the compound-matrix identity, via Cauchy–Binet on
  the two rectangular blocks (with a `finCongr` transport identifying
  the two determinant index types).

Honesty note on tiers: unitarity is stated for matrix-unitary `M`
(`Mᴴ * M = 1`, resp. both identities for the two-sided statement). This
is the finite-mode, matrix form of "one-particle unitary": a unitary on
the one-particle space `Fin d → ℂ` *is* such a matrix here.

## Task 3 — Free Hamiltonian gap, DERIVED: **STANDS**

Same file/commit as Task 2 (**`aaa60c2`**).

For a diagonal one-particle Hamiltonian `h` with eigenvalues
`λ j ≥ 0`, set `freeHamiltonian λ = dΓ(diag λ)` (`freeHamiltonian`,
`onePartDiag`). Then:

- `freeHamiltonian_vacuum` — the vacuum is an eigenvector with
  eigenvalue `0`.
- `freeHamiltonian_basis` — every basis vector is an eigenvector:
  `H e_S = (∑ j ∈ S, λ j) • e_S`.
- `free_gap_dichotomy` — every eigenvalue is either `0` (vacuum sector)
  or `≥ Δ` whenever all one-particle eigenvalues are `0` or `≥ Δ`.
- `freeHamiltonian_eigenvalue_gap` — **the derived gap**: if every
  one-particle eigenvalue satisfies `λ j = 0 ∨ λ j ≥ Δ`, then every
  non-vacuum eigenvalue of `H` is `≥ Δ`.
- `freeHamiltonian_selfadjoint`, `freeHamiltonian_expectation`,
  `freeHamiltonian_expectation_nonneg` — self-adjointness, the
  expectation formula `⟪x, H x⟫ = ∑ T, (∑ j ∈ T, λ j) · |x_T|²`, and
  nonnegativity of expectations.

**Label, precisely:** this is the ladder's first *derived* gap — the
one-particle gap `Δ` is an input, but the Fock-space gap is *computed*
from the operator (as the sum of occupied one-particle energies), not
assumed as a structure field and not inserted by definition. It is a
statement about the **free** theory at **finite modes**. It is **not**
the interacting Yang–Mills mass gap, and nothing in this rung touches
the continuum theory. (Contrast: the 2026-10-14 ACME toy, where `Δ > 0`
is an assumed field and the "gap" is the assumption restated.)

## Task 4 — Quantum Poincaré implementation, finite-mode: **PARTIAL**

Same file/commit as Task 1 (**`1bf2b26`**).

What honestly lifts, proved:

- `gamma_rep` — the general lift: any one-particle unitary
  representation `ρ : G → Matrix` of any group `G` (homomorphism,
  `ρ g` unitary for all `g`) lifts through `Γ` to a unitary
  representation of `G` on Fock space — multiplication and identity
  respected, vacuum fixed, every sector preserved, inner product
  preserved. This discharges the *form* of Rung 6's
  `SecondQuantization_window` (whose three clauses were exactly:
  homomorphism, identity, vacuum invariance — with sector preservation
  and unitarity added here) at finite modes, for any group whose
  action preserves the finite mode set.
- `lattice_translation_rep` — a concrete instance: the lattice
  translation group `Multiplicative ℤ` acting by diagonal phases
  `ρ(k) = diag (j ↦ exp (2πi k j / d))` (`latticeRho`), with
  `latticeRho_one`, `latticeRho_mul`, `latticeRho_unitary` (T1:
  `star (phase) * phase = 1` via `Complex.exp_conj` and
  `exp` additivity). Its Γ lift is a unitary representation of the
  translations on Fock space fixing the vacuum and preserving sectors.
  By `gamma_diagonal_basis`, a basis vector of occupied modes `S`
  picks up exactly the product of the occupied modes' phases — the
  finite-mode shadow of momentum additivity.

What does **not** lift, priced exactly:

1. **The mass-shell one-particle representation.** Rung 6's
   `OneParticleRep_window` stays T5. There is no Lorentz-invariant
   hyperboloid measure / one-particle Hilbert space of a massive
   scalar in Mathlib to restrict to finitely many modes, and this rung
   does not construct one.
2. **Boosts and continuum rotations.** A Lorentz boost (and a generic
   rotation) mixes a finite momentum set into infinitely many modes;
   no finite mode set `Fin d` is preserved, so there is nothing for `Γ`
   to lift at finite modes. The liftable symmetry at finite modes is
   exactly the subgroup preserving the mode set — here: lattice
   translations by phases (and, by the same machinery, any finite
   permutation/phase action on modes).
3. **The infinite-mode / continuum limit.** Passing from `Fin d` to a
   continuum one-particle space needs the infinite-mode Fock
   construction, already priced in Rung 8 (direct-limit / completion
   work, not done here).
4. **Interaction.** `Γ` and `dΓ` are the *free* lifts. No interacting
   Hamiltonian, no gauge field, and no Yang–Mills dynamics appear in
   this rung; the Task-3 gap is the free gap, deliberately.
