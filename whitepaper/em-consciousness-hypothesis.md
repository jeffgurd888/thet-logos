## The hypothesis

neuroscientific and physical paradigm: the brain's endogenous electric and

## 1. Resolving the binding problem

Traditional computational models treat the brain as a vast network of
discrete, point-like neural firings. This raises the binding problem: how do
billions of spatially isolated neuronal spikes seamlessly unify into a
single, cohesive moment of visual, auditory, and subjective experience?

An electromagnetic field naturally integrates information across spatial
volumes at or near the speed of light. Because electric and magnetic fields
are continuous vector quantities, local field superposition automatically
aggregates spatially distributed neural activities into a single, unified
physical state — without requiring a hypothetical central processor or
homunculus.

At the physiological level, an electromagnetic model of consciousness is an
extension of Organised Charge Separation (OCSC). Biological systems
systematically maintain steep local electromagnetic gradients — such as the
~0.18 V potential across mitochondrial inner membranes or the resting
potential across neuronal lipid bilayers — preventing thermodynamic

Under the master equation governing life currents, the divergence of the
life current directly tracks the conversion of local electromagnetic field

charge-separating architecture.

## 3. Ephaptic coupling and recursive feedback

For an EM field to be conscious — rather than just an inert field — it must
exert causal force back onto the substrate that generated it. This occurs
via ephaptic coupling: the endogenous, macroscopic electric fields generated
by synchronous neuronal firing alter the membrane potentials of adjacent
neurons, shifting their likelihood of firing.

This creates a recursive, self-referential loop:

- Neural firing generates local field potentials (LFPs).
- Superposed LFPs form a unified, continuous electromagnetic field
  configuration.
- The integrated field influences downstream neural firing via ephaptic
  feedback.

## Implications if the hypothesis is correct

**Digital vs. analogue consciousness.** Standard silicon-based digital
computers — which operate via discrete, isolated transistor states,
deliberately isolated from one another to prevent crosstalk — would never
become conscious, regardless of software complexity. True synthetic
consciousness would require analogue hardware explicitly designed for
continuous, high-density electromagnetic field integration and ephaptic
feedback.

**Exogenous sensitivity.** If the conscious mind is an integrated EM field,
it should exhibit measurable phase-locking or resonance sensitivity when
exposed to precise, phase-matched external electromagnetic fields — ranging
from focused transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) down to planetary-scale
resonances like Schumann cavity modes.

**Anesthesia mechanism.** Anesthetics may work not merely by blocking
specific chemical receptors, but by dampening the coherent charge separation
required to sustain the brain's macroscopic electromagnetic field —
collapsing the global field geometry back toward baseline thermal noise.

2. **The selection problem.** A lightbulb generates EM fields. What
   distinguishes the brain's field configuration — coherence length,
   recursive feedback depth, algebraic structure — such that one is
   conscious and the other is not?

3. **Binding twice over.** If the quantum state is a single vector in H
   (never separate, nothing to bind) *and* the classical field is
   continuous (automatic superposition), which level carries the
   explanatory weight — or must both be present?

4. **Falsifiability ladder.** What is the cheapest experiment that could
   kill this hypothesis? Candidate: demonstrate full conscious report
   under conditions where macroscopic field coherence is provably
   destroyed but neural firing patterns are preserved.

5. **Toy model.** Can a finite EM-coupled algebra be constructed whose
   modular flow reproduces a binding-like integration measure? This would
   move the hypothesis from T5 toward T4.
