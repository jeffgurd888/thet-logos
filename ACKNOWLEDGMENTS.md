# Acknowledgments — where the true credit goes

This program formalizes. It did not originate the ideas, the problems,
or most of the mathematics it verifies. At the direction of the
author (Jeffrey Michael Gurd, 2026-10-17: "make sure the true credit
goes to them"), this page names the staircase this corpus stands on.

## The problem

- **Chen Ning Yang and Robert Mills** — the gauge theory whose
  rigorous construction is the summit this program climbs toward.
- **Arthur Jaffe and Edward Witten** — the official Clay statement
  of the Yang–Mills existence and mass gap problem.
- **The Clay Mathematics Institute** — for posing it as a Millennium
  Problem.

## The geometry

- **Paul Dirac** — the Dirac operator D at the heart of every
  spectral triple in this corpus; spin, antimatter, second
  quantization, and constrained dynamics all begin with him.
- **Alain Connes** — noncommutative geometry and the spectral
  triple; the distance formula, the spectral action, and the
  framework this corpus formalizes.
- **Ali Chamseddine** — co-architect of the spectral action and of
  the almost-commutative Standard Model geometry.

## The modular theory (heat and time)

- **Minoru Tomita and Masamichi Takesaki** — the modular theory
  that turns a state into a flow: the machinery behind this corpus's
  modular dynamics.
- **Alain Connes and Carlo Rovelli** — the thermal time hypothesis:
  time as the modular flow of a thermal state.

## The axioms and the fields

- **Arthur Wightman** — the axioms a rigorous quantum field theory
  must satisfy; this program's windows are his.
- **Konrad Osterwalder and Robert Schrader** — the Euclidean axioms
  and reconstruction: the bridge this program's continuum pitch is
  priced against.
- **Werner Heisenberg** — the operator language of quantum
  mechanics.
- **Erwin Schrödinger** — the equation whose dynamics this corpus
  formalizes.
- **James Clerk Maxwell** — the first gauge field theory; the
  template every gauge theory since has followed.
- **Albert Einstein** — the field equations whose Hilbert-type
  terms the spectral action recovers at finite level.
- **Isaac Newton and Gottfried Wilhelm Leibniz** — the calculus
  and the limit: the continuum this program works to reach honestly.

## The lattice

- **Kenneth Wilson** — lattice gauge theory and the renormalization
  group picture; the route this program's continuum pitch takes.
- **Shmuel Elitzur** — the theorem that gauge-variant quantities
  cannot acquire expectation values; proved here as a result, not
  assumed as a slogan.
- **Miroslav Fiedler** — the variational eigenvalue bound used in
  this corpus's graph-Laplacian exhibit.
- **Alfréd Haar** — the measure every lattice expectation here is
  integrated against.

## The analysis

- **Laurent Schwartz** — distributions and the function spaces of
  this corpus's analytic rungs.
- **Marshall Stone and John von Neumann** — the spectral and
  unitary machinery underneath the dynamics.
- **Leonhard Euler** — the action-and-extremum tradition the whole
  Lagrangian world descends from.

## The staircase in modern form

- **The Lean community and the Mathlib contributors** — thousands
  of people whose verified library (over one hundred thousand
  declarations) this corpus imports directly. Our theorems stand on
  their theorems, mechanically and literally.
- **The constructive field theory tradition** — Glimm, Jaffe,
  Balaban, and many others, whose decades of work mark the frontier
  this program measures itself against.

## This program's share

Formalization, verification, and the finite-level results proved
here are ours. The concepts are theirs. If this corpus is ever
cited, cite the originators first.
