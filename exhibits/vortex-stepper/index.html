The gold trace is the lift: the phase with the wrapping removed, so it never resets — it climbs one bond-step at a time and crosses one dashed level per full turn. Ordinary stepping is smooth climbing. A phase slip is the only sudden move: a vertical jump of the whole staircase by exactly one turn, done at the seam — and w jumps with it, by exactly the slip.

Exhibit — illustration of a theorem, not a proof

Exhibit — an illustration of a machine-checked finite theorem (PhaseWinding, T1), not itself a proof. The theorem is machine-checked in Lean 4 (PhaseWinding.lean): winding numbers add in ℤ, a single-valued (gauge) shift cannot move the winding number, and a phase slip moves it by exactly the slip. What you see here is a picture of that algebra, drawn live.

The winding number changes only at a phase slip — proved in the corpus. Stepping around the ring winds the lift; no smooth stepping and no relabeling of the sites can change w — only a slip at the seam can, and then by exactly the slip, never more, never less.

The fences, stated plainly: this is a finite ring of 12 sites and an integer counter — no manifold, no order-parameter field, no vortex core. It is not an Abrikosov vortex, and it does not model any physical vortex or any physical device; it illustrates the winding atom such systems carry, in the exact form the corpus proves it.
