Now lighting: precession → sunlight timing

Each link lights for its quarter of the cycle. Precession retimes the seasons' sunlight; the sunlight times the melt; the melt rearranges Earth's mass — its moments of inertia; and the moments are the very handle the Moon and Sun grip to drive the precession. The loop closes.

Our position · the countdowns

Our position

11,700 years into the Holocene interglacial. The natural next glacial inception sits roughly 50,000 years out — and human carbon has postponed it past 100,000. We are early in the interglacial, not late.

Countdown A — the flip in who leads

Antarctica's loss rate overtakes Greenland's: scenario window ~2040–2070, if differential acceleration persists. Assumption, printed: Antarctica's loss keeps accelerating faster than Greenland's. If it doesn't, the window moves — or never arrives.

Countdown B — the day itself

The melt's lengthening of the day overtakes tidal braking: ~2100 under high emissions, when the melt term (~+2.6 ms/century) passes the tidal term (+2.3 ms/century). Scrub this toy to a melt pulse peak and you can watch the toy's melt term cross the tidal line.

Countdown C — the threshold

West Antarctica is a threshold, not a date. Once committed, its collapse would run for centuries. No year is registered for it, and none should be read into this panel.

The fences, stated plainly: the toy's pole migration is magnified thousands of times — the real drift is about a centimeter a year, invisible at this scale, so the dot's walk is a diagram, not a measurement. The tempo shift is capped at the paper's honest ceiling: +0.3%, and that only for melting all modern ice. The flip's fences: the glacial drive in this exhibit moves up to ≈5×10²¹ kg — about 4 ocean masses, about 200× every glacier and ice sheet on Earth — pole-ward; that is beyond any physical melt. And the direction matters: real melt moves the moments the other way — melting everything raises C by +7.9×10³² kg·m² and widens the polar gap; it deepens the stable seat, it does not spend it. The spending half of this clock is ice moving pole-ward, which no melt does; it is drawn so the gear between the two cycles can be seen. The clock is sped up by ~10¹⁰ and overdriven by ~200×; both exaggerations are printed here, on the face. MONO versus DUAL is the asymmetry lesson in the parents' terms: what destabilizes the intermediate seat is the asymmetry the single pole's redistribution works on the moments — in DUAL the equatorial pair is held exactly balanced and the polar gap at Earth today's guard, so no intermediate seat ever forms and the spin rides steady. The body in this toy is perfectly rigid and torque-free. Earth is neither — it flows, its bulge follows its spin, and the Moon and Sun hold torques on it — and every one of those differences settles the real planet further into its seat. One-way mode is the no-return scenario: it breaks the loop on purpose; scrubbed to the end of its drive, the MONO seat is spent past its crossing and the flip switches off there too.
