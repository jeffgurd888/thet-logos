A single walker wanders anywhere — its r² proves nothing. But average 200 independent walkers and the noise cancels: the measured mean tracks the exact line. That agreement is the theorem, seen from the outside.

Exhibit — numerical demonstration, not a theorem

Exhibit (numerical demonstration, not a theorem). The theorem — expected squared displacement equals x² + y² + t exactly — is machine-checked in Lean 4 (DiffusionDefect.lean). What you see here is the simulation agreeing with the proof.

The fences, stated plainly: t is the step count — the independent clock, not physical time. The coefficient 1 in front of t is the walk's chosen diffusivity (one lattice step per tick, uniform over the four directions). The + signs are the chosen Euclidean signature. Change any of those choices and the identity changes with them; within them, it is exact — proved, not fitted.
