# Exhibit — The Rotating Tesseract and Its Shadow

**Exhibit (interactive visualization, not a theorem).**

One 4D cube (tesseract), shown two ways at once:

- **Top panel:** a perspective projection of the rotating tesseract — the
  w = +1 cell in red, the w = −1 cell in blue, the bridges between them in
  gold. It rotates genuinely in all six coordinate planes of 4D space;
  per-plane controls and presets are in the page.
- **Bottom panel:** the flat orthographic "sunlight shadow" of the same
  rotation, the way a wire tesseract's shadow falls on a table.

The counts survive both projections — 16 vertices, 32 edges, 24 faces,
8 cells. The lengths and angles do not; that is the whole lesson of a
projection, and it is printed on the exhibit itself.

Open `index.html` in any browser. Single self-contained file, no
dependencies, no network calls. Free forever under the repository's MIT
license — built by Nexus Research as part of the thet-logos / ACMD
program (machine-verified foundations; the theorems live in `lean/`).
