Exhibit — a picture of the mathematics, not a proof

Labeled plainly: this is an exhibit. Everything on this page is computed on a 23×23 grid — a finite-difference picture of the mathematics, not the continuum chamber. The underlying solver was verified on the bench: against a rectangle, whose discrete spectrum is known in closed form, it is exact to 1.2×10⁻¹⁵; its continuum error shrinks as the grid refines, the way the mathematics says it should.

The ladder cannot be hit exactly by any chamber in this family. The bench gauntlet — a finer 29×29 grid, a 400-evaluation budget, the floor measured four ways — bottomed out at Match ≈ 0.066, with ratios ≈ 2.157, 2.872, 3.725, 4.597, 5.751 against the ladder's 2, 3, 4, 5, 6. A 2D room cannot hum a 1D string's ladder. The floor is the family's, measured four ways. The search on this page works a coarser grid with a smaller budget, so expect it to land near that floor, not under it.

The clock chord is measured data, nothing more. Its ratios are the standard optical-clock frequency ratios (CCL-CCTF recommended values, 2021), entered as T4 input and used here only as a target shape — no claim attaches to them. On the bench grid the former reached Match 0.533 against it: the clocks' chord sits lower than this family's reach — this family's lowest mode ratios do not go down where the clocks live.

The rules are the design. The search may only propose chambers the rulebook allows — width and height in [0.8, 1.3], aspect between 0.5 and 2, corner power in [2, 6], ripple at most 0.15 with at most 8 lobes, walls clear of the solver box. The rules are carried as data and checked for every candidate, the way the knowledge-based design systems carried them.
