Dissipation — the flip-back burn

A perfect rigid body in empty space would flip forever. A real tumbling body leaks energy as it goes — at fixed angular momentum — and sinks toward the lowest energy its momentum allows: rotation about its largest-moment axis, its seat. Here the leak is charged at each flip-back (and on each swing of the trapped wobble once the flips stop): each flip-back, internal dissipation steers the spin toward the largest-moment axis — the seat — by up to the dial in degrees; energy falls out at fixed angular momentum, and the ledger reads the seat cone it buys. The dial:

Flip-back steering — the dial 15° — steering step per return

The choice, stated plainly: dissipation switches itself ON automatically while the guided sequence runs — the flip regime is where this dial belongs. In free play it starts OFF, so the original exhibit is the untouched default; the switch above turns it on there. The steering bites only on the unstable seat, and is charged at a flip-back — every second counted flip, the return — walking the spin toward the seat at fixed L, until the seat cone is spent and the body settles.

The ledger's degrees are measured, never assumed: the seat cone is the angle between the seat axis and L, the seat swing lost is the sum of the seat-cone drops the charges actually measured, charge to charge, and the burn line reads the excess energy that fell out along the way. (For Earth the seat is C, the figure axis itself — so there the seat cone is the very cone the main display shows.)

The 15° is Jeff's parameter, from his reading of the geological record: paleomagnetic true-polar-wander studies propose out-and-back excursions whose returns do not fully close, with residuals of this size — proposed and contested, not settled. Here it is the largest steering step a single return may take toward the seat. The steering MECHANISM is settled physics: a real tumbling body dissipates energy at fixed angular momentum and ratchets toward its stable seat. A perfect rigid body in empty space would flip forever; nothing real does.
