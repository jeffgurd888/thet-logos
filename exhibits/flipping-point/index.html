The water's own momentum — Jeff's question

The oceans are not passengers in this story; they are part of the spinning body. Here is their weight in the ledger, computed, not asserted:

Mass of the oceans1.4×10²¹ kg
The ocean shell's own moment of inertia≈ 3.8×10³⁴ kg·m²
…as a share of Earth's (C−A) gap≈ 14% — already inside today's A and C
Angular momentum of all ocean currents, relative≈ 10²⁵–10²⁶ kg·m²/s
Earth's total spin angular momentum5.86×10³³ kg·m²/s
The currents' share of the spin≈ 10⁻⁸
Ocean push — shove the spin with the currents' momentum 50%

The push is applied magnified about five million times so your eye can see the cone widen — at true scale it changes the tilt by ~10⁻⁸ of a turn and is invisible. Watch what it does and what it cannot do: the wobble gets rocked, the STATE does not change, and the boundary gauge below does not move. The water's momentum rocks the boat. It cannot move the flipping point — the boundary lives in the moments, and the ocean is 10⁻⁸ of the spin.

The needle measures the gap between the spin axis's moment and its nearest neighbour (—). Drag that gap toward zero and the analytic wobble period stretches toward infinity — the freeze. At zero the two moments are equal, the axis's rank changes, and stability is gone. The flipping point is not a place or a date — it is the moment two moments become equal and the spin's axis changes rank.

Earth, in three buttons

Earth today. Moments (A, B, C) = (8.01, 8.02, 8.04) ×10³⁷ kg·m² — the sliders carry these rounded values; the measured gap is (C−A) = 2.64×10³⁵ kg·m², so H = (C−A)/C ≈ 1/305. The spin sits on C, the largest moment: the stable seat. Run the same formula with Earth's real spin and you get the ~305-day Euler wobble (the observed pole wander is the 433-day Chandler wobble — Earth is not rigid, which softens and stretches it).
